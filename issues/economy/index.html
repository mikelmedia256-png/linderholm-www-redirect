The Economy

Managed Growth & Job Creation

North Alabama is the economic engine for our state. We need to manage this growth wisely, creating jobs for folks who are already here. A two-tier economy is in nobody's best interest.

What Linderholm will do

  1. Manage North Alabama's growth so it creates good jobs for the people already living here, not a two-tier economy.
  2. Work on the "quality of life" basics that bring families here, including schools, traffic, housing, and workforce training.
  3. Use his ties to Europe's aerospace world to connect Huntsville with key decision-makers, and push for a serious look at raising Alabama's $7.25 minimum wage.
Alabama's current minimum wage
$7.25

In his own words

Huntsville and North Alabama are the economic engine for the state. Our civic leaders and the Chamber work hard to bring in industry and economic development. Yet there is still work to be done on the “quality of life” issues that attract families.

Schools, traffic, housing all relate to this issue of economic growth, as does the issue of workforce training. We can not rest on our past success.

Aerospace and Europe

The aerospace industry is a key part of Huntsville’s growth. We have partners throughout Europe, and growing up in the United Kingdom, with personal connections in the current U.K. Parliament, gives me a unique advantage in interfacing with these companies. The most important aerospace trade show in the world alternates between Farnborough and Paris each year. I grew up right next to Farnborough, watching Concorde fly overhead as a kid while they developed it, and I speak passable French. The ability to facilitate quick connections with the key decision-makers will be an asset to the city.

Jobs for the people already here

But as we grow, we need to ensure there are jobs for the people already here, especially in entry-level and service sector roles. Otherwise we run the risk of creating a “two-tier” economy. We need to consider a minimum wage that is higher than the current $7.25. That limit itself is already irrelevant for many employers, but the issue is a serious one. Alabama has one of the lowest labor force participation rates in the country. Moving people into the workforce is simply not going to happen at $7.25, with the cost of food, housing, transportation and childcare.

James Linderholm
